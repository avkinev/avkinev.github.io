Illusory Road

True stories of an enlightened bodhisattva and his journey east

Stories 1. The Bodhisattva and the Old Man

Introduction

Once, I learned that a bodhisattva is a person, a disciple of the Buddha, who has attained the highest level of enlightenment—almost as high as the Teacher himself. I read that one such man, or so they say, came from India to southern China and brought the Chinese the knowledge of the Middle Way. He was supposedly the one who started what is called Chan Buddhism, which later made its way to Japan and became Zen Buddhism. And, of course, everybody knows everything about Zen.

It seems to me that the path to enlightenment must have taken up a considerable part of the bodhisattva’s life. So he must have been rather elderly, if not downright old, when he traveled to China. Which means, in turn, that he could not have walked very fast. He must have stopped to rest and recover his strength. He must have met all kinds of strangers and heard all kinds of stories, seen and learned many interesting things. Wouldn’t you think?

That is what I intend to tell those who may be interested. Here are the entirely true stories the bodhisattva heard on his journey from west to east.

1. The Bodhisattva and the Old Man

The bodhisattva was traveling from west to east. He made his way through dense forests teeming with wild predators, across rushing mountain rivers, and through valleys strewn with enormous boulders. One day, on a hot, rocky plateau, he came upon an old man sitting in the shade of a tree. The bodhisattva went over and greeted him. The old man greeted him in return and invited him to sit down and rest. The bodhisattva sat opposite the old man, and they began to talk. They talked about all sorts of things, and understood one another perfectly. Whatever subject came up, they immediately found common ground. You know how it is when you talk to your best friend: he understands you almost at once, and you understand him just as quickly.

At one point the old man suddenly said to the bodhisattva, “How about I tell you how the human soul is put together?” The bodhisattva was surprised. “Of course, tell me. That sounds very interesting.” And the old man began to explain how the human soul is made, how it works, how it feels. Why sometimes a soul longs to soar away as though on wings, while at other times it hides somewhere deep inside, pining in loneliness. He explained it all so well that the bodhisattva listened, spellbound. “The old man speaks well,” he thought. “And he has it right. Just like a bodhisattva!”

“But I am the bodhisattva,” he thought.

And he felt ashamed.
